Dear Editors,

I am pleased to submit the manuscript "Statistical Inference for Series
Systems from Masked Failure Time Data: The Exponential Case" for
consideration as an Original Research article in Lifetime Data Analysis.

The paper derives closed-form statistical inference for m-component series
systems with exponentially distributed lifetimes when the failure cause is
only partially observed. Under a uniform masking model, I obtain:

  - An explicit Fisher information matrix for arbitrary system dimension m
    and masking cardinality w
  - Sufficient statistics reducing the data to 1 + C(m,w) real numbers
  - A closed-form MLE for the maximally symmetric identifiable case w = m-1,
    where the score equations linearize
  - Asymptotic normality with explicit variance formulas
  - A proof that uniform masking maximizes conditional entropy among symmetric
    masking models, with diagnostic informativeness decreasing monotonically
    in w via a data processing inequality

While closed-form MLEs have been derived for specific small systems
(Miyakawa 1984; Lin et al. 1993), the Fisher information matrix, sufficient
statistics, and asymptotic theory have not previously been obtained for
arbitrary m. The closed-form results eliminate the need for numerical
optimization or Monte Carlo simulation for variance estimation, providing
direct insight into the information structure of masked failure data.

Monte Carlo simulations (R = 100,000 replications) confirm the asymptotic
theory at rate O(n^{-1/2}) across symmetric, moderate, and strongly
asymmetric parameter configurations.

This work connects the competing risks and reliability engineering
literatures, both core areas of Lifetime Data Analysis, and I believe
it is well suited to the journal's scope.

The manuscript has not been submitted elsewhere. All simulation code is
publicly available at https://github.com/queelius/expo-masked-fim.
